Interfaces that move
because something is happening

Every state on this page is driven by a model rather than a curve. The button knows the difference between waiting and failing. The list knows the difference between arriving and being unread. The panel below knows exactly when it stopped being current.

Visual component only

Buttons and submission

One control, four states, four reasons

Press and the control compresses on a critically damped spring. Send and a packet crosses it, so waiting has a direction instead of a spinner. Succeed and a mark lands on the same spring. Fail and that spring is given too little damping, so it rings and settles honestly rather than playing a canned shake at you.

Nothing is submitted. No form, no field name, no request.

New states

Arriving and being unread are two different things

The entrance is over in half a second and only helps somebody who was looking. The mark decays over several seconds and is what tells somebody returning to the tab which rows they have not seen. Hover or tap a row and it is marked seen, because attention is the only honest way to clear a marker that means you have not looked at this yet.

Transitions

A change with a definite instant

A crossfade has to show both states at once, which for two dense panels reads as a smear. A packet crosses instead and the content changes under the brightest part, so there is never a frame showing both and never a flash of empty container. The instant is the useful part: it says exactly when what you were reading stopped being current.
